Everybody in this field is already having this conversation. It happens after
seminars, in the last ten minutes of a group meeting, in direct messages
between two people who are not sure whether it is safe to say the thing out
loud. What it mostly does not do is accumulate anywhere.

That is the gap this is meant to fill. Not another benchmark table, and not
another opinion piece explaining to a general audience what a transformer is.
Somewhere that a person who does high-energy theory or quantum gravity for a
living can write three honest paragraphs about how their week actually went,
and have it read by people who understand the calculation being described.

## What we are trying to avoid

Two failure modes, mostly.

The first is the breathless register: the post that is really about AI in
general, gestures at physics for colour, and could have been written by someone
who has never renormalised anything. There is plenty of that already and it is
not useful to us.

The second is its mirror image — the confident dismissal that never gets
specific. "It cannot do real research" is not an argument. "I gave it the
following setup, it produced this, and here is precisely where it broke" is.
Scepticism is welcome here, and we expect a lot of it, but we would like it to
be the kind that names the calculation.

## What we are hoping for

A record. In five years there will be a great deal of writing about what
happened to scientific practice in this period, and almost all of it will be
written retrospectively by people with a thesis to defend. Contemporaneous,
specific, unresolved accounts by working theorists will be worth more than any
of it — including, maybe especially, the ones that turn out to have been wrong.

So: if you have been meaning to write something down, write it down.
[Send it to us]({{ '/submit/' | relative_url }}). It does not have to be long
and it does not have to be finished.
