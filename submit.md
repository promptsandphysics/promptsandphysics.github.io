To submit a post, email **[{{ site.email }}](mailto:{{ site.email }})**. Include
your text, a suggested title, a link to your homepage or institutional page, your
career stage and your institution.

## What to send

Some things we are looking for:

- an account of a specific calculation where these tools helped, or conspicuously did not
- a reflection on authorship, credit and exposition when part of the work was machine-generated
- an argument about what physical understanding is, and whether producing correct steps is any of it
- a critique of how an institution, collaboration, funder or company is handling this moment
- something from your own experience: how your working day has changed, or pointedly has not

This is not a closed list, and none of it needs length. A single paragraph about
something that struck you and that you cannot quite place is enough. Send it.

## Length and format

No minimum, no maximum. A post can be four sentences ending in a question, or a
full essay. Send the text in the body of the email or as an attachment in
something easy to copy-paste — plain text, Markdown or LaTeX source is ideal,
Word is fine, PDF is the worst case.

**Mathematics is supported.** Write it as you normally would: `$g_{\mu\nu}$` for
inline, `$$ ... $$` for display. It renders through MathJax, so most of what you
would write in a paper works unchanged.

Posts do not need to be polished, but please send something you would be content
to see published as-is. The important thing is that it is honest and specific
enough to start an argument.

## AI use

Using these tools to edit, spellcheck or translate is entirely fine and needs no
disclosure. But a post is worth reading because a person thought it, so please
develop the ideas yourself, even where that leaves the prose rougher. If you go
beyond editing, include a note at the end of the post saying how — we will
display it in a marked-off box rather than burying it.

## Crossposting

We are happy to republish something from your own blog or homepage. If it is
more than a couple of months old, please reread it and send a revised version:
claims in this area go stale quickly.

## Who can contribute

Write from your institutional address, or link a homepage or departmental page
that confirms who you are. If you have a homepage we will link it from your
post; we will not publish your email.

Any career stage: undergraduates, master's and PhD students, postdocs, faculty.
People working at AI labs are welcome too. If you do not fit any of those
categories and still want to write, get in touch anyway.

We can publish anonymously if you prefer, in which case we will show only your
career stage and country.

## What happens next

We will confirm we have your submission. We may send comments, but within these
guidelines the final call on what appears is yours. We will give you a rough
window, then a firm date shortly before publication.
